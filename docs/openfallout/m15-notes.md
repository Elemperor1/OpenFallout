# M15 notes (sound)

What the M15 pull requests changed, and what they rest on. Everything below that is a number or a rule was read from the executables (FalloutNV.exe and Fallout3.exe, the addresses are given) or from the data of the games; nothing from the games is copied here. "Listened to by ear" is Jacob's part and has not been done yet: what the Mac rounds can show is log lines and the state of the sound manager, not what comes out of the speakers.

## What was wrong

- `mwsound` was OpenMW's sound manager. A sound record of Fallout 3 or New Vegas (`SOUN`) was played from its file with a volume of 1, a minimum distance of 1 and a maximum of 255: the data of the record (`SNDD`) was read and thrown away, a record that names a folder (half of New Vegas's do) had no file, and the editor-id lookup did not exist.
- The animations of the actors never set a text-key listener, so no key of a `.kf` file made a sound: the `Sound: <editor id>` keys (about 2,550 in New Vegas's animations) and the `Enum: Left/Right/BackLeft/BackRight` keys that the games' footsteps are made from.

## SOUN data

`SNDD` is 36 bytes: the settings (12) and the curve (24). `FalloutScript::SoundData` (`components/falloutscript/sounddata.*`) reads them:

| Bytes | Field | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | minimum attenuation | times 5 units: inside this the sound is at full volume |
| 1 | maximum attenuation | times 100 units (the executable compares a distance with this byte times 100: FalloutNV.exe 0x8384F0, 0x838608); 0 means the sound has no distance |
| 2 | frequency adjustment | percent, signed |
| 4-5 | flags | 1 random frequency shift, 2 play at random, 4 environment ignored, 8 random location, 0x10 loop, 0x20 menu sound, 0x40 2D, 0x80 360 LFE |
| 8-9 | static attenuation | hundredths of a decibel, the whole sound quieter by this |
| 10, 11 | stop time, start time | of the day, in 1440/256 minutes |
| 12-21 | curve | the volume in percent at five distances spread evenly from the minimum to the maximum (the editor's default is 100 50 20 5 0) |
| 24-27 | priority | |

The sound manager now plays a sound record with its own curve: the gain is worked out in the sound manager from the distance of the listener each update (`SoundParams::mDistanceGain`), and the output's own model of distance is turned off for such a sound (rolloff 0). The static attenuation, pitch (with the random shift flag), loop, environment-ignored and 2D flags are used. `OFBase::SoundManager::playFalloutSound` plays a record by editor id or by form; a record that names a folder plays one of its files at random. The script command `PlaySound`, the weapon shots and the footsteps all go through it.

## Footsteps (FalloutNV.exe 0x837CC0, "ImpactMixer")

The animation says a foot came down (`Enum: Left` 0, `Right` 1, `BackLeft` 2, `BackRight` 3: the index in the table of the words at 0x1199B50; the same table has Idle 4, Aware 5, Attack 6, Hit 7, Death 8, Equip 9, Unequip 10, CameraShake 11, StopSounds 12, HitShader 13, RiderSoft 14, RiderLoud 15, Weapon1 16, FoleySneak 17, FoleyWalk 18, FoleyRun 19, ArmorSneak 20, ArmorWalk 21, ArmorRun 22, Auxiliary1-8 23-30). The sound is a `SOUN` record named `FST<Surface><Slot>`:

- Surfaces (0x11A1888): ConcSolid, ConcBroken, MetalSolid, MetalHollow, MetalSheet, Wood, Sand, Dirt, Grass, Water, then ChainLink, Tile, Carpet in New Vegas (Fallout 3: 10 surfaces and 9 slots).
- Slots (0x11A18BC): WalkL, WalkR, RunR, RunL, SneakL, SneakR, StairsL, StairsR, Land, Jump (no Jump in Fallout 3). The key `Left` (value 0) plays the slot named R and `Right` the one named L; the back feet are the front ones (value minus 2). Sneaking, then stairs, then running, then walking decides which.
- The surface comes from the Havok material of what the foot is on, by the table at 0x11A17F8 (36 entries; Fallout 3's at 0xF6C538 has 32): material 0 and the ones past the end are concrete; water when the character is in water (the port's choice: water over a twentieth of its height, about the ankle; the executable's threshold was not read). A surface that lacks a sound falls back through its parent (0x11A17C4: ConcBroken to ConcSolid, MetalHollow to MetalSolid, MetalSheet to MetalHollow, Sand and Grass and ChainLink to Dirt) and in the end to `FSTConcSolidWalkR` (the table is built at 0x839AB0).
- Volume: the player in the first person has the INI's `fPlayerFootVolume` (0.65), everyone else a random volume between 0.9 and 1 (0x476B70); a step is heard no farther than `fMaxFootstepDistance` (1100) from the listener, and the sound's own maximum distance counts too. Power armor adds `FSTArmorPowerL/R`, other heavy armor `FSTArmorL/R` (0x8382A0-0x838386), with a volume one minus a random tenth.
- The Havok material of a collision shape is read from the NIF (`BulletShape::mHavokMaterial`, from the first solid body), that of land from the `LTEX` record of the texture that shows most at the point (`HNAM`), through `OFBase::World::getFloorMaterial`.
- Stairs: the executable asks a bit of the actor's movement flags (0x1000000 of the flags at +0x410 of its process, tested at 0x838750) that its Havok physics sets; the port's physics has no such bit, so a stair is told by its shape (`FalloutScript::Footsteps::isStairs`, the port's own rule): the ground under the foot is a flat tread of an object (the z of its normal 0.95 or more) and the ground 24 units ahead or behind it, along where the actor faces, is 5 to 30 units higher or lower than a slope of that tilt would make it (a ramp rises evenly over the reach, so the rise a tilt of the normal explains is taken off first: a ramp that rises 7 units in 24 is not a stair). The slots of the stairs (`StairsL`, `StairsR`) are chosen for such a tread; running and sneaking come first.

Landing plays the `Land` slot of the surface at any landing of the player (with the hard-landing sound over it when it hurts), and a jump the `Jump` slot (New Vegas only). A jump in armor has the sound of the armor over it (`FSTJumpArmor`, power armor `FSTJumpArmorPower`, the sounds of the games); a landing is the ground's alone.

## Creatures and foley

- A creature's sounds are in its `CREA` record: each `CSDT` (a type, the same number as the `Enum` key) is followed by its `CSDI` sounds with a `CSDC` chance each; `CSCR` names the creature whose sounds it takes when it has none. The text key plays one of them, picked by the chances (a list that comes to less than 100 leaves the rest as no sound). Idle, aware, attack, hit and death keys of the animations play the same way.
- New Vegas armor has `SNAM` sounds (the sound, a chance, three bytes, a type of 17 to 22) and a `TNAM` audio template (`ArmorHeavyAudioTemplate`, `ArmorLightAudioTemplate`, ...) that it takes them from when it has none. `Enum: FoleyWalk` plays the type 18 sound of what the character wears.

## The player's body

The body of the player in the third person stops being updated when it is hidden (first person); its steps come from its animation, so it keeps its update going when it is not drawn (the node mask is `Mask_UpdateVisitor`, which is what the NIF loader uses for hidden nodes). The sounds that the animations name (`Sound:` keys) come from the first-person arms while they show and from the body when they do not, so that none is made twice.

## Weapon sounds and the pad's rumble

- A weapon record has the sounds of taking it out (`NAM9`), of putting it away (`NAM8`), one that goes on while it is held out (`UNAM`, a loop) and that of a blow it blocks (`NAM6`), in both games. The equip sound plays at the `Enum: Equip` key of the move that takes the weapon out (in the arms of the player as in the body of anyone), from where the character stands, at the record's own volume (the executable passes a volume of 1); the loop starts when the weapon is out. Putting the weapon away stops the loop and plays the unequip sound (the move of putting it away is not played: its key is taken for the moment of holstering). The player hears its own weapon in 2D. What a character holds out is not heard once it has gone from the world or is dead, and holding the same weapon out again leaves the loop as it is (the body and the arms of the player both say so, so the one that is shown asks).
- The controller shakes for a blow the player takes (`fRumbleStruckStrength`, `fRumbleStruckTime`: 0.75 and 0.3 s) and for the player's own blow with no projectile, a blow of the hand or of a melee weapon (`fRumbleHitStrength`, `fRumbleHitTime`: 0.5 and 0.3 s; FalloutNV.exe 0x89CEF1 and 0x89CF90, never in V.A.T.S.), besides the pain rumble (`fRumblePainStrength` 0.75, `fRumblePainTime` 0.5 s). `SetRumble` (left, right, seconds) shakes the motors by what a script says. Nothing shakes while a menu is open, not for a script's `SetRumble` either. The defaults are the ones the executable registers (0xF68A7E and the ones after it); the games' files overwrite them.
- Not done, and why: a blow that is blocked has no rumble, and `NAM6` plays no sound, because the port has no blocking (no state of the fight rules blocks a blow, so the sound would have nothing to be played for); the shake of an explosion's camera has no rumble (where the executable gives one was not found); a weapon mod's own shot sounds (`WMS1`, `WMS2`: the silenced shot) are not used, the shot is the sound of the weapon record.

## Music (FalloutNV.exe 0x992D90 combat, 0x591A40 folder picks, 0x830050 / 0x830080 attenuation)

What the engine does now, and what it does not.

**Fallout 3.** A cell names a music record (`XCMO`; `1NoMusic` is silence), a Fallout 3 cell can ask for a kind instead (`XCMT` 1 public, 2 dungeon), and a worldspace has its own (`ZNAM`). The record's `FNAM` ends with a backslash when it names a folder: every `.mp3` in `Data\Music\<folder>` is a track. The pick is the executable's: a random one that is not the last (a pick equal to the last one, other than the first, becomes the first, and a first that follows the first becomes the second), and the first track of a folder starts anywhere.

**New Vegas.** The music is not in the music records of the cells but in the audio markers of the world: a static reference with `MMRK` has a media location controller (`CNAM`, record `ALOC`), a radius (`XRDS`, 5000 or 10000) and the shares at which the layers of the music come in (`MNAM` 0.33, `NNAM` 0.67). The controller names location sets (`LNAM`) and battle sets (`GNAM`) of `MSET` records. 87 of the 93 controllers name location sets; of the other six, four (Goodsprings, Doc Mitchell's house, a small RV camp, Highway 95) keep them in the lists of the faction of the controller (`RNAM`) and two (Vicky and Vance) have none; the NCR and Legion camps have both: `HNAM` for a neutral standing of the faction to the player, `ZNAM` for an ally, `XNAM` a friend, `YNAM` an enemy. A controller with location sets of its own uses those; the ones without take the list of the faction's standing to the player (the same rule as for two characters: what scripts said, else the records), and the neutral list when the one asked for is empty.

A media set has up to six files (`NAM2` to `NAM7`: three layers for the day, three for the night), the decibels each is attenuated by, the boundaries (`JNAM` to `ONAM`, percent of the marker's radius) where the layers change, which of them are on (`PNAM`), fades and waits (`DNAM`, `ENAM`, `FNAM`). The player in a marker's radius hears the innermost layer that is on and whose boundary holds the player's distance. All the layers of the half-day play in step and only the wanted one is audible, so that a layer change is a cross-fade, not a restart. Day starts at 6:00 and night at 23:54 when the controller says `use defaults`, else at its `NAM5` and `NAM6` (256ths of a day).

**Fights.** The music of a fight is on when the enemies that hunt the player are a threat: the player's strength divided by the sum of theirs is under `fCombatMusicPlayerTargetedThreatRatio` (2); or characters nearer than `fCombatMusicNearCombatInnerRadius` (2500) fight with a friend of the player and are stronger than the player (times `fCombatMusicPlayerNearStrengthMult`). While it plays, a hunter keeps it on, and so does a fight nearer than the outer radius (3500). When the fight seems over a timer of `fCombatMusicStopTime` (5 s) starts, and the music stops at the look after it has run out. A look is every `fCombatMusicUpdateTime` seconds (1). The engine counts the strength of a character as its health and counts a fighter only while the cell it is in is loaded around the player (a hunter far away has lost the player). Fights that do not hunt the player are every other fight of the combat state (`CombatState::fights`): a friend of the player is a teammate or a character that the player's faction stands by as ally or friend, on either side of the fight. The music of a fight is the battle media set of the controller when the place has one, or else the battle music of the default objects (`DOBJ`).

**Volume and loops.** A music record's `ANAM` is a number of decibels: its size is how much quieter the track is, a positive one makes it loop. A change of music fades over 1 s (`iMusicTransitionInMS`).

**Scripts.** `PlayMusic` plays the track of a music record (with the record's attenuation and loop). While it plays, the music of the place is quiet and it comes back after it. The title music is `Data\Music\Special\MainTitle.mp3`.

**Loop modes, picks and fades of the sets.** The looping options of a controller (the low two bits of the high nibble of the first byte of its `NAM1`; the bit above them, 0x40, says that the day starts at 6:00 and the night at 23:54) decide how its sets play (FalloutNV.exe 0x598000, read by the updates of location sets 0x597A60 and of dungeon sets 0x598060): option 0 loops the tracks, any other plays them once and the controller picks a set again when they are over, after its own delay (`NAM4`, which is 0 seconds in all 93 controllers). In the data 51 controllers loop and 42 are "random" (1); none says retrigger or none. 30 of the 42 name more than one location set, so for them the end of a track is a new pick. The pick (0x595560) is the executable's: the first set of the controller's list is picked, each one after it takes over with a chance of one in four, and the set that was picked goes to the end of the list (0x905330 and 0x905820), so the set that played last is picked a quarter of the times and the others share the rest (`FalloutScript::Music::pickSet`, the order of each list is kept for the session). A set fades in and out over its own time (`FNAM`, 6 or 9 s; the port used 1 s before), and the layer that is heard changes only when the time of the set (`DNAM`, 6 s) has run since the last change (0x597E04: the executable sets the time of the next change at every start). The executable restarts a looping track by itself a fade before its end, with a cross-fade; the port loops it in the sound output, with no gap and no cross-fade.

**Region sets.** Where no audio marker has music, the sound region that the acoustic space of the cell names (`REGN` `RDSB` and `RDSI`: battle sets and an incidental set) is the music of the place (0x82FB70 asks the controller of the marker first, the region after it). In a fight the region's battle sets are picked like a controller's battle sets, in place of the default battle music (`DOBJ`).

**Open and close sounds.** A battle set and a set of a dungeon play the sound of their `HNAM` (at +0xBC of the loaded record) when they start and that of their `INAM` (+0xC0) when they stop, as the incidental set plays its two (0x598890); the sounds are sound records of the folders of stingers (`fx\mus\...`), heard as music.

**Incidental sets (`NAM1` 3).** While nothing else plays and no fight goes on, the set of the region plays its open sound by day and its close sound by night, one after the other, with a random wait between its times (`DNAM` and `FNAM` by day, `ENAM` and `GNAM` by night: 4 to 16 seconds in the desert set; the executable multiplies them by 1000 and compares milliseconds, so they are seconds); the wait is the executable's one for all the sets (0x11CA9FC); a set whose times are zero would start its sound in every frame, so the port keeps at least one second between two stingers (`Incidental::minimumWait`), and the stingers wait until the layers of the place that was left have faded out. The stingers are short (a few seconds), so in open country with no music of its own they come about every ten seconds: whether that is what the game sounded like is for the ear check.

**Stingers of the default objects (`DOBJ`).** The music of a death, of a quest completed and of a level gained plays once over the music of the place (the track of the record, with its attenuation, whatever its loop flag says: a stinger that looped would never give the place's music back), and the music of the place comes back after it. The executable's callers of these records were not found, so the triggers are the ones their names in the editor say: the player dying (once), a quest that completes, and the player's level going up. The open and close sounds of a set wait while the track of a script has the music, and are heard when it is over.

**`StreamMusic`** reads its string and does nothing (FalloutNV.exe 0x5C2460: the handler parses it into a buffer and returns), so the port registers a handler that does nothing and a script that calls it plays no music.

**What the port chose, because nothing that was read says it:** how long the music of a place rests between two tracks of a Fallout 3 style record (60 to 180 s, a random time; a track that loops has no rest), the delay before the first one (8 s) and after a fight (10 s, the battle sets' wait), the fade of a battle track when its set gives none (3 s). The triggers of the three stingers above.

**Not done:** the marker's own triggers `MNAM` and `NNAM` (the boundaries of the set, `JNAM` to `ONAM`, are used); the cross-fade of a looping track at its end (the port loops it gaplessly).

## Ambient sound (`FalloutNV.exe`, the acoustic space and the region)

**The space.** A cell names an acoustic space (`XCAS`, record `ASPC`). The space has up to four loops (`SNAM`: dawn, day, dusk, night) and, for an interior, only the first is used. The loops change at 5, 8, 18 and 20 o'clock (`fAmbientDayStart` and its kin of the game settings). A loop starts about 2 s after the player is in the space (it is not played in the instant of a door), fades in over 3 s, and fades out over 5 s when the space or the time of day changes to another loop. The loop of the space is a normal `SOUN` record: its flags, volume and file are those of the sound.

**The region.** The space names a sound region (`RDAT` of the sound kind and `RDSD` in the `REGN` record: a sound, the weather it is for, a chance). A sound of the region that loops is on while the weather is one it is for (pleasant `1`, cloudy `2`, rainy `4`, snow `8`: the classification bits of the weather) and fades in over 0.6 s and out over 1 s. A sound that does not loop is rolled now and then; one that asks for a random location (`SOUN` flag `0x08`) is played at a place around the player that is between a quarter and three quarters of how far the sound is heard.

**The weather.** The `SNAM` sounds of the weather in use (rain, wind, thunder) are heard out of doors: those that loop play while the weather is, the others are rolled like the sounds of a region.

**What the port chose, because nothing that was read says it:** how often the sounds of a region are rolled (each sound has its own clock of 120 s that starts at a random time, and it is rolled with its chance as a share of a million when the clock runs out, so the sounds are spread out), the chance of a thunder-like sound of the weather (0.3 per roll), the distance of a sound with a random location. The weather in use is the one the sky shows now (the port does not blend the sounds of the weather it is changing to).

**Files.** 340 sound records of New Vegas (most of the loops: a campfire, flames, water, the hum of a house) name `.wav` files that the archives have only as `.ogg`; the port looks for the `.ogg` when the `.wav` and the `.mp3` are not there.

**Water (FalloutNV.exe 0x4E6A80, the sampler that runs every frame).** A water record names at most one sound (`WATR` `SNAM`: 36 of New Vegas's 83 water types and 8 of Fallout 3's 62, loops such as `AMBWaterLakeLP`). It plays from a place that moves with the player, once the player is near the water of the cell (`FalloutScript::NearWater`, `OFSound::WaterSoundUpdater`; the numbers are the defaults of the `[Water]` settings of the games' INI, `uNearWaterRadius` 1000, `uNearWaterPoints` 8, `fNearWaterOutdoorTolerance` 1024, `fNearWaterIndoorTolerance` 512). Out of doors the sampler looks at 8 by 8 points in a square of 2000 units around the player; a point is wet when the terrain under it is lower than the water level of its cell; half of the points wet is all of the strength, and the player's height above the water takes away from it in a line down to nothing at the tolerance. In an interior the strength is 1 at the surface of the water of the cell and falls in a line to 0 at the tolerance, above or below. Under the water nothing is heard. The sound comes from the player in the direction of the middle of the wet points, as far away as the radius times how far from full the strength is (on the player when all of it is near), at the water level. It fades in and out as any loop does.

**Not done:** the loops of an interior cell that names no acoustic space (25 of New Vegas's 492 interiors and 128 of Fallout 3's 598): in what was read the loops come from the space the cell names and no default for a cell that names none was found, so such a cell has no loop of its own here (the random and retrigger loop modes that the executable has are those of the music controllers, above; the loops of a space are plain loops).

## Radio (`FalloutRadio` in FalloutNV.exe: UpdateStation 0x834260, SetBroadcastState 0x8359E0, StartRadioConversation 0x835BE0)

**What a station is.** A talking activator with the record flag `0x00020000` that is placed in the world with `XRDO` (the reach: a radius, or everywhere, or the worldspace, linked interiors or the cell, a share of static at the outer radius, and the reference the radius is measured from). New Vegas has 49 such records and 29 placements, Fallout 3 28 and 29. `0x10000000` on the record says the station is not proxied for the Pip-Boy (a radio in a casino or a house, heard only where it is placed), `0x40000000` that it broadcasts all the time.

**What it says.** Every radio quest adds responses to one topic, `RadioHello` (form `0x126`, type 7, in both games): the speaker is the station, and the conditions of the responses (the quests that run it) choose what it says. A response leads on through its choices (`TCLT`) to the topics of the station's quest (news intro, stories, transition, music intro, music, and round again), whose responses are flagged random and run immediately and have no conditions of their own but the quest's variables, which the result scripts move. A song is a response whose sound form (`TRDT`) is a sound record with the song's file. The engine says a response, runs its end script, then takes the first of the topics it offers that the station has a response for, and goes on until a response offers none; the next chain starts at `RadioHello` again (`FalloutScripts::radioStep`).

**Settings from the executable.** The update interval 0.25 s (`iRadioUpdateInterval`), the station timeout 1 s (`iRadioStationTimeout`, the default in the executable), the volume 0.5 (`fDefaultRadioVolume`), the mute while somebody talks to the player 0.5 (`fRadioDialogMute`), the static share at the outer radius of a station that names none 0.1 (`fRadioStaticAtOuterRadiusPct`, the default in the executable). The message "%s signal found." (`sRadioStationDiscovered`, the first time) and "%s signal lost." (`sRadioSignalLost`) with the radio tower icon and the sounds `UIRadioSignalFound` and `UIRadioSignalLost`.

**Saves.** A `radio` section of the save (`FalloutScript::Radio::Saved`) keeps the stations that scripts turned on or off and those the player found.

**Pip-Boy.** The radio tab lists the stations that are in reach, proxied and have something to say now, after a first row that turns the radio off (the words of that row are the page's own); picking a row tunes the station (`core.pipboy.radio()`, `core.pipboy.tuneRadio(id)`). The tuned station's voice and songs play as 2D tracks at the radio volume less the static. `PipBoyRadioOff`, `SetBroadcastState` and `GetBroadcastState` (a station on the air from the start when its record says it broadcasts all the time, otherwise when a script says so), `ForceRadioStationUpdate` are implemented. Stations that are not proxied say their responses where they are placed (a loaded cell and in reach), as any character does.

**Volume.** The radio has its own channel of the volume: `radio volume` of the `[Sound]` settings (0.5, the `fDefaultRadioVolume` of the games), a slider in the settings menu beside the music one, and `OFSound::Type::Radio` in the sound manager; the voices and songs of the tuned station play on it as 2D tracks (`SoundManager::startRadioTrack`) at the channel's volume less the static. While somebody talks to the player (a conversation, a topic menu) the radio is as quiet as `fRadioDialogMute` (0.5) says.

**Commands.** `PipboyRadio` (FalloutNV.exe 0x5D7FB0: the word of the first argument turns the radio on, `1`, `enable` or `on`, or off, `0`, `disable` or `off`, or only tunes, `tune`, ignoring case; without a station the radio goes back to the one it was tuned to, and a station that is not in reach leaves it off), `SetRadioToDialogueOnlyMode` (0x5E0020: the radio says what is said and plays none of the songs, and a song that plays is cut off), `StartRadioConversation` (0x5D82A0 calls 0x835BE0: the station that the command is called on says the topic next, the given one or the first of the station; what is said is cut off, and a conversation asked of a station that is not tuned says it first when it is) and `SetNPCRadio` (0x5D8100: a character has a station on, or off, and says what it says where it stands, one line after the other as a character says what it is told to; a character that is not in a loaded cell is not heard, what it was saying stops when its radio is turned off or set to another station, a conversation asked of its station starts once for it and cuts off what it is saying, and the songs are left out of every station that is heard (the tuned one, a thing in a room, a character's) when the radio is set to dialogue only, and a creature is heard within `fCreatureRadioMax` times 1.1 of the player, 0x835271), `StreamMusic` does nothing (above). The commands are in the games' scripts (a byte scan of the compiled scripts finds the opcodes at most this many times: New Vegas 3 `PipboyRadio`, 5 `SetNPCRadio`, 11 `PipBoyRadioOff`, 11 `StartRadioConversation`; Fallout 3 1, 2, 12, 10).

**Waveform.** While a station is tuned the Pip-Boy page shows the waveform in its tile `MM_Waveform` (FalloutNV.exe 0x79C0C0 and 0x79C090): one of three pictures of `sRadioWaveformFileFormat` (`Interface\Radio Wave\Forward`, `Backward` and `Left`) is on it, a new one picked at random every 750 ms, and it moves along by a tenth of its width every 10 ms, going on from the other side when it has run past the whole. The port draws it as the strip of a compass: a window of its own width that moves over the picture; the games' own zoom of the tile (175) was not read.

**What the port chose, because nothing that was read says it:** how the static grows (none at the station, the station's own share at the outer radius, in a line), that the interiors a station reaches are those the doors join (an interior is linked to the interiors that can be walked to without going out of doors, and to the worldspaces their doors open on: a worldspace reach is heard outdoors in its worldspace and in the interiors linked to it, a linked-interiors reach in the cell of the station and the interiors linked to it, which the data places in vaults, houses and casinos), that a station with no response to say is left out of the list, the pause of 1 s after a chain and of 10 s for a station with nothing to say.

**Not done:** a station that keeps broadcasting while it is out of reach (the executable keeps the time of the response that was on at `+0xC` and the time left at `+0x10` of the station, 0x8355BA; here a station that is tuned again starts a chain from `RadioHello`, not at the point of the song that the time would have reached: starting a stream part way in is not in the sound output); the radio's own static loop `UIRadioStaticLP` and the fade-in of the radio when it is turned on (0x8300C0, 1000 ms); for a station that is not heard from the Pip-Boy, the executable's radius of a reference that has a station on is 110 times the maximum distance byte of the station's template sound (0x835252), where here it is the reach of the placement and the voice's own 3D range. The Pip-Boy's radio page was not opened in the running game (a hidden round cannot open it): the list and the picking are written the way the quests list beside it is, and the `core.pipboy` calls are exercised by the round.

- Music: `FalloutScriptMusic*` (the rules: combat, the pick of a folder's track, the pick of a controller's set, the looping options, the incidental waits; components), `ESM4MusicRecordTest.*` (loaders, with `RDSB` and `RDSI` of the region), `OFSoundFalloutMusic*` (files, and the mixer with a stand-in sound: stems that loop or end, `finished`, `restart`), `FalloutScriptCombatCommandsTest.a_faction_stands*`. The round `scripts/openfallout/round/rounds/m15-music.json` logs `Music:` lines (the controller and whether it loops, the set at each place, the track layers and their gains, combat music on and off, the open and close sounds, scripts' tracks) and `Music track N plays <file>` for each file that is started. The round `m15-region.json` stands in open country that no audio marker reaches (New Vegas, 44000 west and 4000 north of the origin) and logs the stingers of the region's incidental set, then fights two characters there and logs the region's battle sets.
- The engine logs `Footstep of <ref>: material N, <surface>, <slot> -> <sound>` for the first 40 steps (the round `m15-steps.json`; `m15-stairs.json` walks four ways over two stair objects of the world and logs the `Stairs` slots where the rule finds a tread: `ConcSolid, StairsR -> FSTConcSolidStairsR` on the retaining-wall stairs).
- Ambient: `FalloutScriptAmbient*` (the rules, components), `ESM4MusicRecordTest.regionReadsItsSounds` (the loader), `FalloutScriptNearWater*` (the water sampler's rules). The round `scripts/openfallout/round/rounds/m15-ambient.json` logs `Ambient:` lines (the space the player is in and its region, each loop that starts or fades out, each sound that is played); `m15-water.json` stands over the lake of the east and logs `Water:` lines (the sound of the water that fades in and out).
- Radio: `FalloutScriptRadio*` (the reach, static, finding and tuning rules, the commands' words and the creature reach, components), `ESM4PlacedRecord*` (the XRDO loader), `FalloutScriptPresentation*` (`SetRumble`, `PlayMusic`). The round `scripts/openfallout/round/rounds/m15-radio.json` logs `Radio:` lines (the stations the world has, those found, the one that is tuned, each response that is said) and `Music track N plays` for the voice and song files.
